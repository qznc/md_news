# Elevator Inefficiencies: Why Algorithms Matter for Your Daily Commute  

The frustration of waiting endlessly for an elevator is a shared human experience, yet the systems behind these delays are remarkably complex and often riddled with hidden inefficiencies. **The Legacy of Simple Algorithms**  
Elevator systems have long relied on basic algorithms like SCAN and LOOK, which were developed decades ago. The SCAN algorithm moves elevators linearly from basement to top floor and back, while LOOK improves this by reversing direction only when needed. However, these methods prioritize simplicity over adaptability. In the *john.fun/elevators* article, the LOOK algorithm’s rigid structure becomes a liability during peak traffic, such as morning rushes in office buildings, where wait times spike due to inefficiencies in handling concentrated demands.  

**The Rise of Smarter Systems: RSR and Beyond**  
Modern systems like Otis’ RSR algorithm introduce nuanced optimizations. RSR dynamically scores elevators based on estimated time of arrival, passenger load, and directional alignment. For instance, it penalizes elevators already heading to a crowded floor or rewards those idle near a call. This adaptability reduces wait times by ~15% compared to LOOK in high-traffic scenarios, as highlighted in the same source. However, RSR’s success isn’t universal. In smaller buildings or low-flow situations, simpler algorithms may perform better, illustrating the need for context-aware solutions.  

**Destination Dispatch: More Information, Less Flexibility?**  
A counterintuitive twist emerges with Destination Dispatch (DD), where kiosks direct users to elevators based on their exact destination. While DD provides precise data, it often backfires.
