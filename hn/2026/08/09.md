# Apple App Store Review Controversy: AI-Generated App Clone Sparks Debate

The controversy began when Godier launched a web utility called Dark Hours, designed to help users identify visible celestial objects. Godier redirected his domain to the original project and deleted plans for an iOS version.

Adding complexity to the saga, Godier had previously claimed Apple rejected his app for including astrology features, later publishing what he said was a scientific astronomy tool. This led to coverage by [John Gruber of Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2026/08/retraction_app_store_rejection), who later [retracted his endorsement](https://daringfireball.net/2026/08/retraction_app_store_rejection) after learning Godier hadn’t disclosed the app’s original astrology components. The incident underscores rising tensions around AI-generated content, developer ethics, and the reliability of attribution in an era of rapidly evolving tools.  

Several commenters called for transparency, urging Godier to release the AI prompts and commit history to clarify how closely his project mirrored [DarkHours.app](https://darkhours.app). As of now, those details remain undisclosed, leaving many skeptical of his explanation. The episode serves as a cautionary tale about the risks of over-reliance on AI tools without sufficient oversight or verification.
