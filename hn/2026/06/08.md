# The Great Code Erosion: How LLMs Are Redefining Software Engineering Expertise

The rise of large language models is fundamentally reshaping what it means to be an expert software engineer, as a decade’s worth of hard-won domain knowledge in areas like finance and distributed systems debugging is rapidly becoming commoditized by AI assistance. The professional identity built on deep specialization—once a career moat—is now being challenged by tools that can replicate, and in some cases surpass, human expertise in these very domains, prompting an existential questioning of long-term value and employability.

What once required years of hands-on experience in PCI compliance, double-entry ledgers, and payment idempotency can now be synthesized by AI given sufficient context and documentation.

The final remaining bastion—code quality and architecture, often reduced to "taste"—is also under pressure. The meticulous craft of writing maintainable, well-structured systems is being devalued in a world where code is increasingly treated as disposable and LLM-maintainable.
