# S&P 500 Shuts the Door on SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic

The S&P 500 has decided to keep its rules intact, denying fast-track entry to SpaceX and effectively blocking OpenAI and Anthropic from early inclusion, dealing a blow to their plans for rapid access to passive investment capital.

SpaceX had requested accelerated entry into major stock indexes ahead of its historic IPO, but S&P Dow Jones Indices rejected the proposal after a month-long consultation. The company refused to waive key requirements, including the 12-month "seasoning period" for new listings, the need for at least 10% of shares to be publicly available, and the mandate for consistent profitability over five consecutive quarters. SpaceX’s current unprofitability and its plan to float only about 3% of its shares made it ineligible under existing rules. The decision also affects OpenAI and Anthropic, which were poised to benefit from similar rule changes after their expected IPOs [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/06/sp-500-blocks-fast-spacex-entry-wont-waive-rule-for-unprofitable-ai-firms/).

The stakes were high: Bloomberg Intelligence estimated that fast inclusion in the S&P 500 could have triggered $14 billion in passive fund purchases for SpaceX, $8 billion for OpenAI, and $4.6 billion for Anthropic. These funds automatically buy shares of companies in the index, reflecting its $7.5 trillion in assets under management. However, S&P only relaxed rules for smaller indexes like the S&P Total Market Index, leaving the flagship S&P 500 unchanged [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/06/sp-500-blocks-fast-spacex-entry-wont-waive-rule-for-unprofitable-ai-firms/). In contrast, Nasdaq and FTSE Russell have already adjusted their rules to allow faster entry for SpaceX.

The denial comes as SpaceX faces scrutiny over its valuation. Morningstar recently labeled the company as "significantly overvalued," valuing it at $780 billion—less than half of its $1.75 trillion IPO target—citing the strength of its Starlink satellite service while questioning its aggressive spending on AI and speculative projects like orbital data centers [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/06/sp-500-blocks-fast-spacex-entry-wont-waive-rule-for-unprofitable-ai-firms/).
