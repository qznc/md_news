# Leveling the Playing Field: How Randomized Reservations Are Changing High-Demand Product Launches

The traditional approach to launching limited-availability products is being challenged by a more equitable method that spreads access over multiple days. This randomized system aims to reduce the advantage of bots and instant reactions while creating fairness in procurement [central url](https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/45479024/view/685257114654870245).

High-demand product launches have historically relied on first-come-first-served models that favor automation and technical advantages. Users with bots, fast internet connections, or quick reflexes typically secure products before general consumers can react. Valve's Steam Machine launch represents a shift toward randomized reservation systems designed to eliminate these unfair advantages [Steam Machine announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/45479024/view/685257114654870245).

The randomized approach works by accepting reservations over several days without rewarding early submissions. Instead of racing to click at launch time, interested buyers submit interest forms during an extended window. The system then randomly selects winners from qualified applicants, removing the urgency that disadvantages casual shoppers.
