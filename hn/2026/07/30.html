Google DeepMind Unveils Gemini Robotics 2 With Full-Body Humanoid Control

Google DeepMind has released Gemini Robotics 2, a suite of models designed to give robots whole-body coordination, fine dexterity, and the ability to collaborate on multi-step tasks. The announcement marks a shift from stationary manipulation to mobile humanoids that can walk, crouch, and manipulate objects simultaneously. Researchers claim the system can adapt to new robot bodies in hours rather than months.

The release comprises three distinct models. Gemini Robotics 2 is a vision-language-action (VLA) model that translates visual input and language commands directly into motor controls for full humanoids and bi-arm platforms. Gemini Robotics ER 2 serves as a high-level reasoning agent that plans multi-minute tasks, coordinates with the VLA, and manages multi-robot teamwork. Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 is a compressed VLA optimized to run locally on robot hardware without cloud connectivity.

DeepMind demonstrated the VLA controlling three different platforms—Apptronik Apollo 2 with two distinct hand types and a Franka Duo dual-arm setup—using a single model checkpoint. The system performs whole-body mobile manipulation, such as walking to a shelf and placing an object, and dexterous skills like tying knots with a 22-degree-of-freedom hand. The On-Device variant adapts to new embodiments like the Dexmate or SO101 platforms with under 200 demonstration examples in a few hours.

Safety infrastructure accompanies the capability jump. DeepMind introduced ASIMOV-Agentic, a benchmark for agentic safety orchestration that tests a model's ability to refuse unsafe actions and request human help when uncertain. Gemini Robotics ER 2 shows improved detection of human proximity and triggers safe stops per collaborative robot standards. A detailed safety technical report is available.

Discussion on Hacker News surfaced several themes. A DeepMind researcher noted the lab's unique breadth spanning frontier models, open weights, and robotics. Commenters debated the latency of LLM-based control loops, with some arguing 1-2 second response times are impractical for real-time actuation compared to traditional PID controllers. Others questioned the humanoid form factor, citing actuator limitations, safety risks in homes, and the viability of specialized non-humanoid automation for manufacturing. Societal impacts arose, including labor displacement concerns and the desire for eldercare assistance. Several users expressed skepticism about cloud-dependent robots in private homes, emphasizing the importance of the On-Device model for privacy.
