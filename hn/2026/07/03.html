Alibaba Blocks Claude Code After Backdoor Concerns

Company cites alleged security risks and demands removal of the AI‑powered coding tool from employee devices, signaling a shift in China’s approach to foreign tech in the workplace.

In a recent internal memo, Alibaba instructed staff to uninstall and replace Claude Code, an AI‑assisted development platform, after security analysts flagged potential “backdoor” vulnerabilities in the software. The move follows a broader crackdown on foreign artificial‑intelligence services that handle sensitive codebases, as Chinese regulators tighten controls over data and intellectual property.

The decision was driven by two key factors. First, a security assessment revealed that Claude Code’s integration with Alibaba’s cloud infrastructure could expose proprietary algorithms to unauthorized access. Second, the company’s compliance team identified a conflict with the “Secure Software Development” policy that prohibits the use of external tools deemed “high‑risk” in critical development cycles.

Alibaba’s statement emphasized that the priority is to protect its source code and maintain strict control over the development environment. “We are committed to safeguarding our intellectual property and ensuring our engineers work within a secure, compliant framework,” the company said in a brief press release. It added that alternative in‑house development tools will be rolled out to minimize disruption.

For developers accustomed to the convenience of AI‑driven code suggestions, the change will require a period of adjustment. The company recommends migrating existing workflows to its proprietary IDE extensions, which have been vetted against the new security standards.

The ban also reflects a wider trend in China’s tech sector, where regulators increasingly scrutinize foreign AI products for potential data leaks or state‑sponsored exploitation. Similar actions have been reported at other large enterprises, indicating that Alibaba’s move may become a precedent for how Chinese corporations approach AI‑assisted development in the near future.

While the immediate impact on day‑to‑day project timelines is likely to be manageable, teams will need to re‑train and adapt to the new tooling landscape. The long‑term effect could reshape how Chinese firms develop software, leaning more heavily toward domestically built AI solutions.
