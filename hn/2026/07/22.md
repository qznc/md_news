# Passkey Usability Challenges in Multi-Device and Multi-Browser Environments  

The Hacker News discussion around passkeys reveals widespread confusion among experienced users about how to use them consistently across devices and browsers, highlighting a gap between technical design and real-world usability. Despite being promoted as a secure, passwordless future, many users struggle with basic questions like device portability, backup options, and cross-platform compatibility.  

One long-time tech professional with 26 years of experience questioned whether setting up a passkey on one device would allow login from others, especially when using multiple browsers like Brave, Firefox, and Safari across iPad, iPhone, Windows, and macOS systems. They expressed concern about being locked out if they accidentally created a passkey on a less-used browser and wondered how many passkeys could be registered per site, ultimately choosing to stick with LastPass due to its predictable behavior.  

Others echoed similar frustrations, noting that even those familiar with public-key cryptography find passkey workflows unclear when switching between devices or operating systems. A parent shared that while physical U2F keys were easy to explain to elderly relatives as "house keys," passkeys lack that tangible simplicity. Meanwhile, some users reported success using password managers like 1Password or Bitwarden to sync passkeys across devices, describing the experience as "just works" once properly configured—though they acknowledged this approach introduces reliance on third-party vaults.  

Family account sharing emerged as another pain point, with users asking how spouses or children could access shared services like Amazon Prime or Pandora if passkeys are tied to individual devices or biometrics. One user managing multiple family accounts said passkeys made emergency access nearly impossible without compromising security practices.  

Concerns about vendor lock-in were also raised, particularly regarding device attestation features that could restrict passkey usage to platforms approved by Google, Apple, or Microsoft. Critics warned this might force users into Big Tech ecosystems unless the specification limits such controls.  

Despite these challenges, supporters argued that passkeys improve security by eliminating phishing risks and reducing password fatigue, especially when integrated smoothly with trusted password managers. The debate underscores a central tension: while passkeys offer strong theoretical benefits, their real-world adoption hinges on solving usability issues for non-technical users navigating complex, multi-device lives.
